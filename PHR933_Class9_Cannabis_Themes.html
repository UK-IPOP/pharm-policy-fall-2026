What students have seen of cannabis in Kentucky pharmacy

“In a couple of sentences, describe any real-world pharmacy experiences that you’ve observed related to the expansion of cannabis products in Kentucky.”

Responses could be coded to more than one theme, so the counts add up to more than 96. Select a theme to read its examples.

What stands out

  • More than a third of the class (36 of 96) said they have not seen cannabis come up in practice, and 14 said only that. Several connected this to Kentucky’s program being new and to patients keeping use private.
  • Among the 60 who did describe something, the most common experience was patients asking about drug interactions, alongside CBD sold over the counter in community pharmacies.
  • Several students said pharmacists lack the evidence or regulation they need to counsel with confidence. One named a gap in practice: nobody is clearly responsible for monitoring interactions with medical cannabis that never passes through a pharmacy.
  • Only 6 students mentioned FDA-approved cannabinoids. One said they had filled Epidiolex for a long time before learning in this class that it is cannabis-based.

The themes in students’ words

No direct experience yet

36 of 96

Students who have not yet seen cannabis come up in pharmacy work. Many expect it to arrive during their careers.

I have not personally encountered any real-world cannabis experiences, but I am sure by the time I am in my career that I will most definitely encounter patients that either use cannabis or are wanting to incorporate it into their therapy.
This could be due to recreational use being illegal in Kentucky, making patients who use it medically keep it more private.

Drug interactions & counseling questions

29 of 96

Patients asking whether cannabis is safe with their medications, and pharmacists counseling on sedation, anticoagulants and CYP metabolism.

I actually had a patient this summer on my IPPE that was prescribed cannabis but was taking Eliquis. She had read somewhere there was a drug interaction and was asking about it.
Many pharmacists have questions about who is responsible for monitoring patients using medical marijuana for potential drug interactions if these are not “prescription” medications and not being dispensed through a pharmacy.

OTC CBD / hemp products in the pharmacy

23 of 96

CBD oils, gummies and topicals on community pharmacy shelves, visits from vendors, and customers asking about them.

It was a huge headache for inventory (theft), and most of the products were constantly out of date. There was also the issue with inconsistent dosing, where patients would complain about batches not being effective.
Typically, patients share that they have heard the benefits of CBD relieving joint pain and inflammation.

Screening, disclosure & stigma

18 of 96

Asking about use during medication histories, patients becoming more open, and others hiding use for fear of judgment or legal trouble.

The patient mentioned that they were looking for melatonin because they were going to a new doctor and did not want to tell them about their cannabis use for sleep.
Now that it is more accepted and medically legal in Kentucky, patients are more open to talk about it than before, which allows pharmacists to fully assess a patient.

Dispensaries, medical program & access

18 of 96

New dispensaries, medical cannabis card ads, patients asking the pharmacy for medical cannabis, and pharmacy owners deciding not to take part.

When the first dispensary opened in Bowling Green, we would have people come to the retail pharmacy and ask when they would be able to get medical marijuana from us.
He noted that he can see the act doing good things for some people, but he did not want to become a dispensary and decided not to complete the application.

Regulation, quality & evidence gaps

11 of 96

Delta-8 sold at gas stations, inconsistent dosing, recalls, unknown ingredients, and too little evidence to counsel from.

The lack of regulation also increases the potential for drug interactions because the actual ingredients and concentrations in these products may not always be known.
My pharmacy sells CBD oils and gummies but just recently in July they were recalled. We had to take all of the product off of our shelves.

Observed use & clinical harms

9 of 96

Impaired patients at the counter, cannabinoid hyperemesis, ER admissions and use during pregnancy.

I have seen several patients at the VA who are admitted for cannabis-induced hyperemesis. Veterans are using cannabis for PTSD, sleep, pain, and nausea, but end up overusing and causing hospitalization.
Some patients with diagnosis of COPD and other breathing issues consistently use cannabis, which negatively affects patients.

Youth safety & look-alike edibles

8 of 96

Edibles that look like candy, accidental ingestion by children, and counseling on safe storage.

To people who are not aware of what cannabis is, the only warning sign is the triangle cannabis icon on the package and it may not be obvious.
For patients with known use of medical cannabis, the pharmacist will be sure to reiterate the importance of proper storage and to keep it out of reach of children.

Prescription cannabinoids (dronabinol, Epidiolex)

6 of 96

Dispensing dronabinol for appetite and nausea, and Epidiolex for seizure disorders.

I have seen this in various patients on chemotherapy who didn’t respond well to other agents such as megestrol, cyproheptadine, or mirtazapine.
At my job at UK Specialty Pharmacy, we fill Epidiolex regularly and I never realized that it was a cannabis based product until today.

How this was coded

I read all 96 submissions and coded each one to one or more of nine themes. To check that grouping, I ran an NMF topic model (TF-IDF, unigrams and bigrams). With only one to four sentences per response, the statistical topics were noisy. They still recovered the same four largest clusters: no experience yet, OTC CBD, interactions and counseling, and dispensaries and access.

Quotes are lightly trimmed for length and shown without names. The per-student coding is in the accompanying spreadsheet.
